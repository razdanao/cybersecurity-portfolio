# Government Cybersecurity Publications and Why They Matter

Study notes in my own words.

## 1. Examples
| Publication | Publisher | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| [National Cyber Threat Assessment 2023-2024](https://cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/national-cyber-threat-assessment-2023-2024) | Canadian Centre for Cyber Security | An assessment of the cyber threats facing Canadians and Canadian organizations |
| [National Cyber Security Action Plan (2019-2024)](https://www.publicsafety.gc.ca/cnt/rsrcs/pblctns/ntnl-cbr-scrt-strtg-2019/) | Public Safety Canada | The government's plan for cyber security, including a coordinated police response and a national way to report cybercrime |
| *Enabling Distributed Security in Cyberspace* | U.S. Department of Homeland Security (2011) | A vision for automated, collective cyber defense across organizations |

## 2. One finding that stands out: the National Cybercrime Coordination Unit
The Action Plan describes a **National Cybercrime Coordination Unit (NC3)**, operated by the RCMP, to coordinate Canadian police operations against cybercriminals, advise police on digital investigations, produce cybercrime intelligence, work with the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, and provide a **national public reporting mechanism** for individuals and businesses to report cybercrime and fraud. It stands out because cybercrime was long under-reported and handled by many separate local forces; a single coordination point and one place to report addresses both gaps.

## 3. Why these publications matter to working professionals
- **A shared view of the threat:** they show which threats are rising (ransomware, supply-chain attacks, state-sponsored activity), so budgets and attention go to what matters.
- **Risk assessment input:** they supply real-world likelihoods and examples for your own risk register.
- **Policy and compliance direction:** they signal where regulation and expectations are heading.
- **Practical guidance and benchmarks:** many include controls, checklists and reporting steps.
- **Common language** for talking with management, regulators, auditors and other organizations.
- **Incident reporting:** they say who to call, which affects legal duties and evidence.

## 4. Reading them critically
Publications age quickly, so check the date. Government reports and vendor reports have different perspectives and biases. Combine several sources and verify claims against technical data.
